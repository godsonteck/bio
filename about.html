A little more about me.

I’m Emmanuel Drah. I grew up in Dzodze and now live in Ho, Ghana. This is the story of where I started, what I’ve learned, and the experiences that have shaped me.

I’ll tell it myself.

“The most important thing I learned has nothing to do with a classroom. It came from standing at a counter and paying attention to people.”

Emmanuel Drah · Ho, Ghana

My interest in technology started in childhood, long before I had the means for it. I did not own a computer. There was no money for one. And the elders around me had never been exposed to technology, so they could not understand what I kept reaching for. Nobody was against me. They simply did not know. So I stopped trying to explain, and taught myself instead, quietly, one long night at a time. That is how I became an introvert, not by choice but by circumstance. The habit of sitting alone with a problem until it finally makes sense is still how I work today.

I studied at Ho Technical University, earning my HND in Information and Communication Technology. But the education I am most proud of comes from watching how people actually live and work, and figuring out how to be genuinely useful to them.

I served at NHIS Dzodze during national service, working in the records department (MIS) and at the public relations desk (P.R.O). I kept member records straight, guarded the details people trusted us with, and spoke for the office to everyone who walked in confused or worried. It was my job to send them out calmer and clearer than they came. That mattered to me more than the title.

I work at the reception desk of NS Luxury Villa. That means greeting guests who arrive tired, answering the same questions patiently for the hundredth time, staying calm when someone is frustrated, and making sure every person who walks through that door feels taken seriously. I have been doing it long enough to know that real, patient presence is rarer than people think.

The road behind the work.

2024 to Present

Receptionist · NS Luxury Villa

Every day, I meet people from different places, with different moods and different stories. My job is to make each of them feel at ease, whether they are checking in smoothly or standing there because something has gone wrong. I have learned that calm, clear communication is a real skill, and I have had to earn it daily.

2023 to 2024

National Service · NHIS Dzodze

I was assigned to the MIS and P.R.O departments. I managed health insurance records, kept the office’s information in order, and served as the public-facing voice of the office: explaining policies, answering questions, and making a confusing process feel manageable for the people sitting across the counter.

2020 to 2023

Ho Technical University

I studied HND in Information and Communication Technology. Beyond the technical skills, university pushed me to be self-directed and to take initiative. I showed up. I did the work. I finished.

2018

A standard I chose

In 2018, I wrote down the kind of person I wanted to become: someone who puts action above the comfort of only dreaming. I have carried that promise into every room since.

The promise I keep.

The phrase Success Above Dreams started as a private standard I chose in 2018. The company now has its own home, but the original idea is still personal: do the work, stay consistent, and be the person you said you would be even on the days when nobody is watching.

“This isn’t about becoming someone else. It’s about becoming fully who you already are, and doing it on purpose.”

The standard I work to.

Choose to act.

A dream is a wish until you move. Success isn’t luck or timing. It’s the decision you make today, small or not.

Give yourself permission.

Don’t wait for someone to approve of you before you begin. Nobody is coming to hand you the green light, so you give it to yourself.

The work nobody sees.

The unglamorous kind of work, done daily, with no audience. Applause comes later, if it comes at all.

Stay rooted.

My root is Ewe, from Dzodze, and that carries into the work wherever it goes. Ambition that forgets where it comes from has nothing to stand on.

“Xevi dze atizigba dzi la, meŋlɔa anyigba be o.”

An Ewe proverb from home: Even when a bird perches high on the tallest tree, it never forgets the ground it came from.

Resilience.

Hard seasons don’t cancel the dream. They test how much you meant it.

The personal part

I grew up without a father. Nobody taught me how to carry myself, how to hold a standard, or what a man is supposed to look like from the inside. I had to figure that out alone, earlier than most, and quietly. By the time I was old enough for people to expect it, I had already been living it. A man in a boy’s body, not because I chose to grow up fast, but because there was no other option.
